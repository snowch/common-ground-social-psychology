An attribution is an explanation of what caused an action.   A dispositional attribution locates a cause in characteristics of the person, such as goals, attitudes, or enduring tendencies. Leo’s claim that [Maya](/mˈaɪə/) did not care is a dispositional explanation.   A situational attribution locates a cause in circumstances, such as demands, constraints, or available opportunities. The broken lift and helping a neighbor offer situational explanations of the delay.   Personal characteristics and circumstances can contribute together. [c01s04b]  The fundamental attribution error describes a tendency to give too much weight to personal characteristics and too little to circumstances when explaining another person’s behavior. Its strength varies with culture and context.   Correspondence bias is a related, more specific idea: inferring an enduring disposition from behavior that can be explained by situational constraints. The terms overlap, but they do not identify every possible error in a causal explanation.   Leo treats [Maya’s](/mˈaɪəz/) late arrival as a sign of low commitment before investigating what delayed her. The broken lift and helping a neighbor make that conclusion less secure. These circumstances do not establish that personal characteristics played no part.   The café story illustrates a possible inference to examine. It does not measure an attribution bias. [c01s05]  Attribution research examines how people explain the causes of actions and events. Those explanations do not, by themselves, settle the cause of one person’s action.   The actor–observer hypothesis proposes that people explain their own actions more through situations and other people’s actions more through personal characteristics. Leo citing a bus delay for his own lateness but [Maya’s](/mˈaɪəz/) priorities for hers illustrates this hypothesis.   Actor–observer differences are not a general rule. A meta-analysis did not support a general asymmetry across positive and negative events; results varied with the event and research method.
