# Whole-course review — v0.2.0

Reviewed 11 November 2026. Scope: all ten chapters, 80 slides, 20 decisions and 60 feedback variants, 20 recall prompts and answers, ten field exercises, the 150-scene recording manifest, conceptual illustrations, player, standalone chapters, PDFs, and portable packaging.

The course provides a coherent short introduction to reasoning about social behavior. Its strongest features are the continuous fictional setting, observation-versus-explanation theme, qualified treatment of famous findings, and low-stakes field practice. Its largest weaknesses are limited depth and questions that often reveal the preferred answer through their alternatives. Completion does not establish mastery.

This was an internal editorial, selected-evidence, instructional-design, and software review. It was not an independent academic review, systematic literature review, learner pilot, or human listening review. No empirical claim of learning effectiveness is made.

## Findings and actions

| Priority | Finding | Action in this release | Remaining limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High | Two multiple-choice decisions per chapter cannot assess the stated analysis and design goals. Some distractors are conspicuously poor choices. | Added six written tasks in a different setting, suggested reasoning, and a four-dimension rubric. Tasks require mechanisms, alternatives, evidence, future consequences, outcomes, and limits. | The original decisions remain introductory formative practice. The new case is self-assessed, unvalidated, and does not certify mastery. |
| High | Methods, competing theories, and evidence receive limited treatment in an 80-slide introduction. | Retained an explicit introduction-only scope; linked selected foundational papers and reviews alongside open reading. | Further reading and substantially more study are needed for depth. The coverage remains introductory. |
| Medium | Selecting an option immediately exposed feedback, making it easy to skip explaining a choice. | Added an explicit **Check choice** step after selection. Unchecked choices cannot queue spoken feedback. Added **Play feedback** directly after checking. | The learner must actually make the explanation; the page does not collect or grade it. |
| Medium | The displayed “full transcript” omitted spoken choices and some recording-specific wording. | Built the recording transcript from the production `say` field, with pause/phoneme markup removed. ## Chapter evidence and teaching review

The following decisions were checked against the teaching text and selected source records, abstracts, accessible articles, or publisher summaries. They are narrower than a full research synthesis. `course-evidence.json` records the specific notes and links used in the learner view; `learning-check.json` is the assessment source.

| Chapter | Scientific and teaching judgment | Additional note / final-case alignment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Situations and attribution | Distinguishes observation, personal explanation, and situation. Does not treat a bias label as proof or deny personal responsibility. Culture/context and repeated observations are acknowledged. | Gilbert & Malone review; case task 1 asks for competing explanations and discriminating evidence. |
| 2 — Evidence and experiments | Correctly separates random assignment from sampling, confounding from an observation, and significance from importance. The 12/100 versus 15/100 figures are explicitly invented. | Assignment balances causes on average, not necessarily in one small sample; spillover and representativeness remain concerns. Case task 2 requires allocation, outcome, spillover, uncertainty, and population limits. |
| 3 — Social cognition | Availability, confirmation, and motivated reasoning are useful distinctions. Records and denominators are emphasized without promising immunity to bias. | The availability heuristic can also be useful. A complaint channel discovers faults without estimating prevalence. Case task 1 adds comparable arrival records and a denominator. |
| 4 — Self-justification | Separates dissonance theory, particular demonstrations, and sunk-cost decisions. The Vaidis multilab null result is limited to its tested high-choice/low-choice prediction. | Do not treat one null result as refuting every dissonance claim. Case task 4 separates defending a past judgment from irrecoverable expenditure and compares future consequences. |
| 5 — Conformity and authority | Normative/informational influence and compliance/obedience are distinguished. Classic experiments are not presented as universal rates or inevitable behavior. | Independent judgments and escalation routes are practical proposals to evaluate. Case task 3 asks why public agreement can conceal different motives. |
| 6 — Persuasion | Processing depends on motivation/ability and the decision; norm claims need evidence. Clicks, attitudes, comprehension, and retention are separated. | Elaboration is a continuum; a cue's role depends on context. Case task 5 requires an honest invitation and a meaningful outcome. |
| 7 — Groups | Polarization, loafing, coordination loss, and groupthink are kept distinct. Poor output alone does not diagnose low effort. | Theories must point toward observable processes, not retrospectively label every failure. Case task 3 asks for coordination alternatives and a procedure. |
| 8 — Prejudice and discrimination | Beliefs, attitudes, treatment, and procedures are distinguished. No individual diagnosis from one implicit score. Access and follow-up are emphasized. | Contact evidence varies by population, intervention, outcome, and follow-up. The 2019 review's gaps are historical findings, not a claim that no later studies exist. Case task 5 requires a barrier, outcome, and limit on attitude inference. |
| 9 — Relationships | Familiarity, opportunity, disclosure, and responsiveness are conditional influences, never entitlement to closeness. Correlation and sample limits are stated. | Mere exposure can be nonlinear; more encounters do not guarantee greater liking. Case task 6 requires responsiveness and respect for choice. Attachment is mentioned too briefly for applied classification. |
| 10 — Helping and aggression | Diffusion and pluralistic ignorance are distinct. Crowds can also support helping. Laboratory aggression measures and practical safety guidance are qualified. | Fischer's meta-analysis supports moderation by context rather than a deterministic crowd rule. Case task 6 requires a specific low-risk helping plan and explanatory limit. Aggression and cooperation receive only brief introductory treatment. |

## Learning design and visual review

The [Instructional Design skill](https://github.com/joelrkohlman/instructional-design/blob/main/SKILL.md) informed the objective-to-task alignment, explanation before feedback, retrieval, and explicit evaluation limits. The [Frontend Design skill](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md) informed readable line lengths, quiet controls, visual hierarchy, keyboard focus, and mobile behavior. The established Bookman/Nimbus type system and café illustrations were retained.

The final case changes from a physical café to an online language club. It asks learners to generate an explanation and a test rather than recognize the nicest-sounding option. Rubric dimensions are concept/mechanism, evidence/uncertainty, action/evaluation, and limits/autonomy. All ten chapter objectives have a related task, but six tasks do not sample every subtopic.

The slides already use short paragraphs, explicit antecedents, and deliberate recording pauses from the v0.1.2 narration review. No narration or voice was changed in this release: `af_heart`, speed 1.0, and all existing pauses remain. Interactive exercises wait for a response; continuous chapter audio has fixed thinking holds and then supplies suggested answers. Learners using the continuous tracks may need to pause longer themselves.

Some scholarly endpoints blocked automated requests, produced a generic intermediate page, or could not resolve from this runtime. Their identities/claims were checked separately using scholarly search and accessible publisher records; the HTTP log is not evidence of full-text review.
- Offline checks verify all embedded media hashes against the manifest, serve each portable HTML document locally, then disable network access for playback and interaction. All ten chapter decks play under those conditions. Direct `file:` navigation is blocked by managed Chromium policy and was not tested. Release packaging checks ZIP integrity and publishes a checksum.

Automated Chromium tests do not establish Safari/iOS compatibility, complete WCAG conformance, accurate screen-reader reading order, or learner comprehension. No audience research, baseline/post-test comparison, or transfer outcome has been collected.

## Next evidence needed

For private self-study, try the new case without notes, use the rubric, and revisit a week later with a genuinely different example. The next quality step is an independent social-psychology accuracy review plus observation of a few intended learners completing the course. To develop a deeper curriculum, expand the methods, competing explanations, cultural/generalization evidence, and assessment coverage substantially, and collect learning/retention evidence. That is a curriculum expansion, not a claim this review can establish.
