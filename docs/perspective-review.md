# Whose judgment is being examined?

Editorial review, 12 November 2026. This pass addresses unmarked changes between checking your own interpretation, assessing someone else’s reasoning, and applying a research finding to a particular person. It covers all ten chapters: 80 slides, 60 feedback variants, 20 recall prompts and suggested answers, ten field exercises, ten evidence notes, the six-task transfer case and rubric, and all 48 visual explanations.

## Editorial decisions

Keep one reasoning task per paragraph. Name the observer and the person being observed. Separate behavior from its proposed cause, and observed results from private motives or experiences. Mark the move from a fictional example to the learner’s own practice. An explicit comparison of perspectives remains useful; an unexplained switch is the problem.

The first attribution concept slide now focuses entirely on checking your own explanation. The next evidence slide marks the separate task of assessing another person’s interpretation. The confirmation-bias lesson follows the same division. Their companion notes use separate paragraphs for the two tasks.
