# Prompt clarity review

Reviewed all 80 slide scripts, 20 decision prompts and options, ten recall answer sets, ten field exercises, and the six written case tasks. The review asks: whose reasoning is under examination, which event supplies the evidence, what must the learner do, and when does the task move from fiction to personal experience?

The opening previously asked “What did you observe?” after describing Leo’s interpretation of Maya. This could mean checking the story, recalling the learner’s own reaction, or adopting a future habit. It now gives two explicit tasks in separate paragraphs: examine Leo’s observation and inference about Maya, then choose a recent personal experience and distinguish the learner’s own observation from an inferred cause. It never assumes the learner made Leo’s inference.

Corrections:

- Chapter 1 opening: name Leo and Maya, identify the story task, then mark the move to personal reflection.
- Chapter 1 second concept: describe Leo’s observation and proposed cause before explaining how the learner can test an interpretation in everyday life. The old wording attributed “Maya doesn’t care” to the learner.
- Chapter 1 recall and suggested answer: ask what Leo observed and inferred; answer with his actual observation and inference. Spell out “personal explanation” as an explanation based on usual tendencies.
- Chapter 2 recall: ask about the attendance increase after Leo’s poster change, rather than implying that all before-and-after evidence is insufficient for every purpose.
- Chapter 3 recall: ask what total Leo needs to estimate a repair failure rate. A vivid case itself does not “need a denominator”; an estimate of frequency does.
- Chapter 5 opening: locate the equipment-check instruction in the later chapter decision and identify the chair and volunteers, rather than posing an unbounded question about challenging authority.
- Chapter 10 recall: specify that the learner is reviewing the café’s response to the fallen shelf and must explain how each of two earlier ideas would improve that review.
- All ten recall screens: answer each prompt, then give one personal example illustrating one answer. The former “Use your own example” left the number and purpose of examples unspecified.
- Written case closing task: identify the newcomer, the unfinished resource list, the requested response and plan, and what kind of uncertainty the learner should state.

Other prompts already name their story participants, define a new hypothetical situation, or explicitly ask for a learner-selected field example. Their wording is retained. The assessment remains formative: clarification does not establish learner mastery.
