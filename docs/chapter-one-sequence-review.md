# Chapter 1: review of alternative visualizations

Every Chapter 1 alternative was reviewed against the action the learner should be able to perform. A page should have one teaching task and one primary illustration. The chapter now uses short sequential steps, with independent narration and Next/Previous controls. It does not require the learner to discover essential teaching inside an expanded diagram.

| Previous placement | Decision | Learning purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Person–situation model | Retain as the overview | Define personal qualities, disposition, situation, and interaction. |
| Alternative: disposition and immediate demands | Promote to a worked-example page | Apply the model to one usually patient volunteer facing increased demands; compare occasions before inferring a lasting tendency. |
| Alternative: observation and attribution | Make it the primary illustration on the observation page | Distinguish Leo’s observation of Maya’s arrival from his proposed cause. This comes before naming an attribution error. |
| Fundamental attribution error on the same observation page | Give it a separate page | Explain excessive weight on personal qualities, with the missing circumstances and context qualification visible. |
| Actor–observer comparison | Retain as its own step | Compare one observer’s explanations of their own and another person’s actions. |
| Procedure for checking an explanation | Retain its recently added independent step | Describe, compare, check alternatives, and revise confidence. |

The worked example remains part of the same topic, rather than becoming a new chapter. Its purpose is to apply the overview, not introduce another label. The observation page establishes a prerequisite for the attribution-error page. Simply moving the two old figures without revising the text would have left those teaching tasks mixed together; the four affected recordings and slide points were rewritten around their specific tasks.

The [Instructional Design skill](https://github.com/joelrkohlman/instructional-design/blob/main/SKILL.md) informs this sequence: explanation, concrete worked example, distinction, application, retrieval, and field practice. The original chapter outcome and its two application decisions remain relevant. Existing progress, choices, and bookmark identities are preserved.

The [Frontend Design skill](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md) informs the visual treatment. Retain the field-guide palette and typography. The worked example now shows disposition and immediate demands as possible contributions to an observed action; dashed paths avoid presenting an untested cause as established. The observation diagram uses teal for the recorded action and plum for the proposed cause, with a clock and question mark. Short labels and generous spacing replace the large generic comparison boxes. The attribution-error illustration retains its meaningful size contrast between person and situation.
