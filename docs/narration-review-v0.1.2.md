# Narration review: v0.1.2

Reviewed all 149 clips after the approved opening story. The revision changes 134 clips and retains 15 short feedback clips. Chapter 1’s approved opening recording is preserved.

## Findings and changes

- **Distant references.** Feedback now identifies the selected action. For example, “This improves comparability” becomes an explanation of why randomizing invitations for one event improves the comparison.
- **Dense explanations.** Stories and explanations separate observations, competing causes, definitions, and implications into short paragraphs. The poster example distinguishes the attendance increase from the claim that the poster caused it.
- **Definitions available only on screen.** Narration now states the meanings of stereotype, prejudice, and discrimination; compliance and obedience; central and peripheral processing; and other terms that previously depended on the slide’s labels.
- **Assumed context.** Recall answers now identify the concept they explain. Field practice separates the immediate task from the later revisit plan.
- **Pacing.** Additional silence follows authored shifts in meaning, separates quiz options, and gives recall prompts thinking time. Short feedback that was already clear is retained.

These are listening edits rather than an external scientific literature review. Core topics, citations, learning outcomes, and correct-answer positions are preserved. Examples and explanations are clarified, including a more precise distinction between the poster’s observed attendance and its uncertain causal contribution.
