# Narration and pacing

Apply these choices when editing a passage; they are editorial guidance, not an automatic course-wide transformation.

- Keep the belief being examined explicit. After introducing another person or event, repeat “Leo’s conclusion—that Maya didn’t care” rather than relying on a distant “his explanation.”
- Explain a technical term on first use, then give a short original example when the definition alone would remain abstract. Distinguish adjacent concepts using the same situation where possible.
- Connect the explanation back to the people and actions in the story. Make an important reasoning step explicit instead of asking the listener to supply it.
- Treat an example as an illustration of a possible process. Keep qualifications about uncertainty, boundaries, and how widely research findings apply.
- Use visible paragraph breaks for shifts between observation, hidden circumstances, interpretation, and reflection.
- Give story beats roughly 0.5–0.7 seconds of additional silence when a break helps comprehension.
- Allow roughly one second at a causal turn or after an important distinction.
- Leave 1.5–3 seconds after a reflective question. Longer exercises remain under the learner’s control; continuous chapter tracks already add six seconds before suggested answers.
- Add pauses at meaningful boundaries rather than after every comma. Keep the original af_heart synthesis speed at 1.0.

Store `[pause 0.6]` and similar markers in a scene’s `say` field. Keep them out of the canonical `text`, so captions and transcripts remain readable. The recording skill inserts silence rather than speaking the markers.
