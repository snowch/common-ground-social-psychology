# Course-wide academic content and optional research

The Chapter 1 approach now extends to Chapters 2–10. All 48 teaching pages have a collapsed **Research behind this** disclosure, while the concepts and qualifications needed to understand the lesson remain in the visible text, narration, and slide PDF. The chapter evidence sidebar also starts collapsed throughout the course. Reading research detail is optional; no additional research pages are inserted into the required sequence.

The course retains 98 steps and 168 recordings. Chapter 1's content, source notes, and exercise remain intact. The nine remaining transfer exercises now ask learners to distinguish plausible explanations, select relevant evidence, interpret an outcome, or identify what a proposed intervention can establish. All ten chapters have a locally saved written reasoning field, with feedback available after a deliberate **Compare reasoning** action. These are formative activities, not a validated assessment of mastery.

## Chapter decisions

| Chapter | Academic focus and source examples | Transfer reasoning and important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Retain the existing attribution definitions, Jones–Harris and Gilbert–Malone discussion, and Malle qualification. | Compare the same volunteer across matched early shifts; keep the original small-record and observational limits. |
| 2 | Define confounding more precisely and explain p-values without treating them as hypothesis probabilities. Link OpenStax methods, Cumming, the ASA statement, replication work, and preregistration guidance. | Separate invitation design from prior interest through allocation within contact groups. More observations do not repair an unfair comparison; assignment does not establish representativeness. |
| 3 | Link availability, confirmation bias, motivated reasoning, and limited-information models to distinct sources. A useful shortcut is not automatically an error. | Define a failure outcome, follow comparable repairs, and supply the denominator. A remaining session difference does not establish its cause or diagnose a person's bias. |
| 4 | Separate dissonance theory, classic induced-compliance tasks, and the specific Vaidis multilab comparison. Distinguish irrecoverable investment from future consequences. | Ask whether retraining changes a future cost comparison. A past nonrefundable payment does not become recoverable by continuing; benefits and uncertainty still need assessment. |
| 5 | Define compliance and obedience through behavior, distinguish informational from normative influence, and retain contextual limits on classic studies. Psychological-safety field evidence is identified as observational. | Compare private estimates, new evidence, and public agreement. The resulting pattern can support an account without proving a private motive. |
| 6 | Distinguish attitudes, intentions, attention, understanding, and later behavior. Link the elaboration likelihood model, norm research, and attitude/intention–behavior syntheses. | Compare truthful message versions using relevant outcomes. Clicks or liking alone do not establish informed or sustained participation. |
| 7 | Define social loafing relative to an appropriate comparison, and separate it from coordination loss. Link minimal-group research, recategorization, polarization, loafing, and the groupthink review. | Inspect effort, handovers, and dependencies. Low output does not establish low commitment; a before-and-after change leaves other explanations to examine. |
| 8 | Distinguish beliefs, attitudes, treatment, access rules, contact interventions, and implicit measures. A procedural change describes an intervention, not a psychological theory. | Follow comparable applicants through relevant stages and ask about barriers. Organizers' impressions are not access outcomes, and changing applicant composition limits causal claims. |
| 9 | Define reciprocity as contingent responses, including negative reciprocity. Distinguish actual/perceived similarity, exposure, responsiveness, and adult-attachment measures. | Examine repeated reports from willing participants. Responsiveness and closeness may influence one another; an association or attachment label cannot decide a particular person's wishes. |
| 10 | Define cooperation as joint action toward shared or mutually beneficial outcomes. Distinguish diffusion of responsibility from pluralistic ignorance, and aggression from anger. | Address an ownership gap in a fictional low-risk stock-list task. A named owner is a proposed application to evaluate, not a validated emergency-response intervention. |

The original pre-explanation decisions remain introductory formative prompts. The later comparison tasks require a rationale and a limitation instead of merely selecting the most considerate-sounding response. Every alternative receives feedback explaining what it could inform and why it is more or less direct for the stated question.

## Evidence presentation

The research disclosures identify the kind of source and what it supports: an experiment, observational field study, meta-analysis, theoretical framework, methods article, statistical statement, or conceptual account. They distinguish the following:

- Published findings from fictional café examples.
- A theory from the results of a particular procedure or measurement.
- An association from a causal effect.
- A research pattern from a prediction or diagnosis about one individual.
- An authored application from an intervention whose effectiveness has been demonstrated.
- An ethical boundary from an empirical result, such as a repeated-exposure curve.

The 2024 dissonance note preserves the tested high-choice/low-choice null result while acknowledging that another comparison was not null. The contact notes distinguish a broad synthesis from narrower randomized evidence and its historical gaps. The implicit-association note retains a relationship with behavior while explaining methodological variation and limits on individual inference. The helping notes explain contextual moderation without turning crowds into a deterministic cause of inaction.

`qa/course-academic-sources.json` records 43 selected source identities used for Chapters 2–10. Publisher/Crossref identities were checked and available abstracts inspected. Linked introductory textbooks support standard concepts and classic task summaries. No claim is made that every full text was reviewed, that the selection exhausts current literature, or that this is an independent academic review. Original abstracts and working source records remain outside the published course and tracked source report.

## Definitions and applications

Glossary entries now define the concept before suggesting a response. For example, cooperation is not defined as a roles-and-feedback checklist, reciprocity is not equated with kindness, social loafing includes its comparison, compliance concerns changing behavior in response to a request, and a p-value is conditional on a model. Existing definitions of attribution, observation, correspondence bias, and actor–observer differences are preserved.

Seven teaching recordings change to carry the corresponding definitions and distinctions. The 36 remaining revised recordings are the nine transfer scenarios and their three feedback variants. Research details remain additional reading and are not inserted into the default narration.

The course has not tested its explanation-checking, invitation, recruitment, coordination, or helping procedures as interventions. Sources concerning a related process do not validate the entire practical package. Essential limitations remain visible in the core lesson and feedback; the disclosures provide the study detail behind them.
