# Chapter 1: academic concepts with optional research detail

This revision implements the reviewed Chapter 1 mock and the subsequent decision to make research detail optional. The existing eleven-step chapter remains intact. Technical definitions and qualifications necessary to understand a claim stay in the lesson, its narration, and its slide PDF. The six teaching pages now have a collapsed **Research behind this** disclosure for study methods, findings, interpretation, limitations, and source links.

## Teaching decisions

- Observation is distinguished from causal explanation. Dispositional and situational attributions receive explicit definitions, examples, and glossary entries. They classify candidate explanations rather than establish which explanation is correct.
- Fundamental attribution error and correspondence bias are related without being treated as interchangeable names for every error in causal reasoning. The text distinguishes overweighting personal characteristics from inferring an enduring disposition from constrained behavior.
- The correspondence-bias disclosure describes Jones and Harris's assigned/chosen essay task and its attitude-inference finding. Gilbert and Malone's review supplies conceptual context. The fictional café illustration is explicitly identified as an application, not an experiment measuring a bias.
- The actor–observer hypothesis is qualified in the core explanation: the cited meta-analysis did not support a general asymmetry across positive and negative events. The 173-study count, methodological qualifications, and source remain optional. The illustration cannot establish a general rule.
- The suggested explanation-checking procedure is described as an authored application. No claim is made that this course has demonstrated it to eliminate attribution bias.

The chapter's evidence sidebar also starts collapsed, including on its evidence page. The visible core explanation carries the qualification, while readers who want more can open the local disclosure or the chapter reading section. Opening research does not change the slide or narration queue. In a stacked layout, the controls remain below the text and above the illustration.

The disclosures travel inside the standalone and offline chapter decks. Their contents also appear in the study companion for reading and printing. The slide PDFs retain the short core lesson and essential qualifications with compact source links, rather than expanding every optional research section onto the slides.

## A stronger application task

The transfer exercise uses an explicitly invented record of six shifts. Noor's three late shifts coincide with both an earlier start and bus cancellations; the three on-time shifts have a later start and normal buses. The learner chooses which additional comparison best separates the competing explanations.

All three alternatives can be informative for some question. Feedback explains why comparing Noor across early shifts with and without cancellations addresses this question most directly, while comparisons across activities or volunteers leave different conditions changing. The exercise retains the limitation that the comparison is observational and the original record is small.

A written reasoning field asks what the comparison keeps similar, what it varies, and what remains uncertain. The response is stored only in the browser when local storage is available and is not automatically graded. Typed text remains escaped when redisplayed. Selecting an option does not expose feedback until **Compare reasoning** is pressed.

The saved-response metadata is shared across standalone chapter files, so opening another chapter preserves a current Chapter 1 response.

The replaced exercise has a revision identity. Old saved choices for its earlier options are cleared so they cannot silently acquire a different meaning. Existing visits, bookmark identities, resume positions, and choices for other decisions remain valid.
